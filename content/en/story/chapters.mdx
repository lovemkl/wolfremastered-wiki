The Wolf Among Us Remastered packages the complete five-episode season — the original 2013-2014 run, rebuilt — into one continuous experience. This chapter guide maps the whole arc: what each episode does, where the case turns, and the decisions that define each chapter. Structure notes first, spoilers second: the first section is safe for new players; per-episode summaries below it discuss events openly, so first-timers should read each chapter's section after finishing that episode rather than before.

## The Shape of the Season

Five chapters, each named with a fairy-tale echo that doubles as a noir beat — **Faith** (the street-level opening), **Smoke and Mirrors** (the investigation widens), **A Crooked Mile** (the trail leaves Fabletown), **In Sheep's Clothing** (the confrontation takes shape) and **Cry Wolf** (the reckoning). The pacing is a classic mystery's: episode one plants the body and the world; two and three complicate both; four strips the protections off the truth; five delivers the confrontation the whole season aimed at, then asks what Bigby — and you — are willing to do about it.

Each episode runs two to three hours on a first playthrough and ends on a turn engineered to make starting the next one feel unavoidable. The remaster integrates them seamlessly — one save, one continuous Fabletown — which is the format the story always wanted: episodic in structure, novelistic in effect.

## Episode One: Faith

The season opens in the Woodlands, Fabletown's luxury building, with a domestic disturbance that introduces the entire cast of the case in miniature: the Woodsman drunk and violent, a woman named Faith on the wrong end of it, Bigby's badge meaning different things to everyone in the room. The hour that follows teaches the game's whole grammar — examination, conversation, timed decisions and violence as a choice — and ends with the discovery that turns a sad night into a murder case: Faith's head, left on the Woodlands' doorstep. The episode's defining choice is its final beat: with the prime suspect in reach, Bigby decides whether the wolf comes out — and the season watches.

## Episode Two: Smoke and Mirrors

The investigation formalizes: Ichabod Crane takes personal charge of the case in all his cowardly glory, the trail runs through Fabletown's seams — a strip club called the Pudding & Pie, the tattooed book of the town's secrets, a bar where the community drinks its memory away — and a second victim lands before the episode ends, raising the count and the stakes. Smoke and Mirrors is the season's interrogation episode: the game hands you rooms full of people lying for reasons, and your read of each one shapes what the case can become. Its defining choices concern who Bigby protects and who he burns getting to the next name.

## Episode Three: A Crooked Mile

The trail points out of Fabletown, and the season widens to match it: A Crooked Mile carries Bigby beyond the community's borders in pursuit of the figure now holding the case together — a name, at last, behind the glamour economy's worst trade. This is the episode where the season's themes concentrate: what people sell to stay hidden, what hiding costs, and what the town's truce is actually for. Its defining sequence is a chase with consequences — failures and choices in pursuit ripple directly into who is standing for the endgame.

## Episode Four: In Sheep's Clothing

The protection comes off. With the opposition identified, In Sheep's Clothing builds the confrontation: alliances get tested, the community's complicity gets named, and Bigby assembles what he needs for the final act from people who each want something different from how it ends. The episode's defining choices are its quietest — conversations that determine who stands with the sheriff when the wolf finally hunts — and its ending points every remaining thread at one room, one confrontation, one decision.

## Episode Five: Cry Wolf

The reckoning. Cry Wolf resolves the Crooked Man's grip on Fabletown's underworld in a finale that alternates full flight and full weight — the chase the season promised, then the trial it earned. The ending variations live here: who survives, who answers for what, and above all what Bigby does with the wolf inside him at the moment the whole town is watching. The choices guide breaks down the finale's variations; what belongs on this page is the shape — an ending that lands because five chapters built it, and that players carry for years precisely because it asked them to decide, under the clock, who the Big Bad Wolf is when it counts.

## Playing the Season in the Remaster

Two remaster-era notes for the run. Presentation: pick your look — original color or Noir Mode's black-and-white — before episode one and enjoy not thinking about it again; second runs in the other mode are the natural replay. And pacing: the episodes are built as evenings, and the remaster's seamless packaging means the only schedule is yours — but the cliffhangers are load-bearing, so stop where the chapters stop.

## FAQ

**How many episodes are in The Wolf Among Us Remastered?** Five — the complete original season: Faith, Smoke and Mirrors, A Crooked Mile, In Sheep's Clothing and Cry Wolf, all included in the one package.

**Are the episodes the same as the 2013 originals?** In story, choices and structure, yes — the remaster rebuilds presentation (visuals, performance, Noir Mode) around the original season rather than altering it.

**Which episode is the best entry sample?** Faith — deliberately: it teaches the systems, opens the case and ends on the hook. If the first hour of Faith works on you, the season will.

**Can I replay individual chapters?** Yes — chapter-select replays with the remaster's integrated save structure are the standard way to chase alternate choices and ending variations without a full second run.
